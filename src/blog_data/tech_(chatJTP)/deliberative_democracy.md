- been my latest obsession

motivation: 
- on a high level, seeing polarization and frustration with democratic systems -- a natural [way to address this] is to help people feel more represented + engaged. help ppl present their views and understand the views of others. in hopes that this will make them feel a part of a system + represented by it, rather than 'against' it or solely on the receiving end/facing the consequences. 
- hope is that if ppl feel more heard -> will be less resentful/disenfranchised -> more engaged -> resulting in systems more representative + less polarized. also that if we have non-extreme/click-baity platforms for ppl to share their views and read the views of others, that ppl will be able to hear other perspectives more clearly + not in such an agressive us vs you way. 
- also, seeing concentration of power in tech companies -- ai policy has moved relatively slowly and has not been without hitches in the EU especially (eg. delays in technical standards, impending enforcement deadline but still w lack of clarity). the result is that a lot of 'governance'/decisions that some would argue should be made by policymakers are being made in tech companies -- eg. in responsible scaling policies, etc. or, some are being made by the US government (eg. banning of fable), but not following any structured document/policy that has gone thru house of reps or senate -- not following traditional democratic procedures.

- the lack of proper democratic deliberation in the field means power is just becoming more and more concentrated
-- the consequences of this in a field with a blast radius as big as AI are huge. AI can shape how ppl think + work + live. 
- without it, every part of the ai lifecycle from development -> which models get deployed -> how they are regulated -> which failure modes get surfaced. are all missing participation from the ppl who are most affected by it at the end of the day. 
-- this is true for AI (and where I noticed it, since thats what I work on), but is also true in many other areas. eg. my prior work with inlight where students were not involved in student mental health research. basically my workbook was advising mental health researchers on how to facilitate student engagement -- be it thru town-hall type meetings + advisory committees and/or thru longer-standing roles on their research teams.
-- similarly, many of these tools are being developed with democracy in mind specifically. I would argue this pattern exists _both_ within traditional democratic systems (eg. policy decisions) but also anywhere where power can become concentrated. 

diff types of deliberative tools:
- trying to build consensus (eg habermas)
- trying to help ppl articulate their own views better (not 'changing' them, but helping them better understand + articulate them) (eg https://blu3mo.substack.com/p/people-dont-know-what-they-want)
- 'mapping' ppls views (eg polis) 

some dicotomies
- deliberation vs mass participation (as explored by helene landemore in: https://www.law.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/Helen%20Landemore%20Can%20AI%20bring%20deliberative%20democracy%20to%20the%20masses.pdf)
- another dichotomy is the voluntary vs implicit gathering of this information. some platforms try to deduce what ppl believe using the vast amounts of info available about all of us online (possibly accurate, but not very participatory == and favours those who already have loud voices in those spaces). vs voluntary platforms like polis where ppl go on and share their views w the explicit purpose of contributing to a democratic process (more participatory, ppl feel more heard)
- amplifying voices vs quietly 'merging' them (see: https://www.noemamag.com/democracy-needs-friction-to-function as a critique of the habermas machine) ---- aka identifying axes of consensus + disagreement vs trying to build consensus. 
-- i personally believe the ladder gets a bit sus when its mediated by tech. need to be really careful. even if not intentional, AI can influence what ppl think a lot in very subtle ways. risks reinforcing / amplifying existing systems + power structures rather than empowering ppl to question + form their own opinnions about which parts of existing systems are good and which shoudl be changed. IMO is the part that humans should be facilitating. 

perhaps an ideal flow in my mind is to: use technical tools to help us listen better + understand / surface axes of agreement and disagreement --> facilitating human conversations to come to a decision on the best path forward.
-- tech can be used to direct our attention efficiently, towards the aspects that deserve our time + debate. 
-- but the actual debating and building consensus (or not necessarily consensus, but coming to a decision that can be considered genuinely representative) should still happen between humans. this is how we connect with one another + build common ground. these are the muscles we need to keep strong -- making spaces to hear other ppls arguments, consider them, adjust your position accordingly. ppl need control over this step to properly feel heard.
- theres a fine line bw helping ppl articulate their own views better (tbf, most people don't fully know/understand their own view from the start) and shaping ppls views. be very careful about toeing this line. 

using tech in democratic processes requires infrastructure. 
- polis in taiwan is an example on a larger scale
- i believe its important to have technical ppl involved who understand deeply the technologies used in the former part. if tech is to be used to identify axes of disagreement, summarize views, etc. we _must_ know what it is doing + capable of, and what it is not. 

makes me wonder how something like this could be used in referendum in alberta, canada. referendums are notorious for ppl voting a certain way without perhaps fully appreviating everything that comes with that side. tools like those used by team mirai in japan (https://www.techpolicy.press/japans-team-mirai-uses-tech-to-bolster-democracy-not-undermine-it/) have tried to use tech as a way to get their message out/help ppl understand what their platform is saying, and also to surface questions + areas of concern. 

being careful not just to say 'AI' -- AI can and likely will play a role in this. its known to be good at summarization tasks (CITE).
